SUBJECT WISE TOPICS

Detailed Subject-Wise Syllabus

The RUHS Medical Officer examination syllabus is primarily based on the standard MBBS curriculum as recognized by the National Medical Commission (NMC). Below are the core subject areas heavily tested in the examination:

1. General Medicine (Highest Weightage)

  • Cardiology: MI, ACS, Heart failure, Arrhythmias, Hypertension, Valvular heart disease, Infective endocarditis
  • Respiratory: Asthma, COPD, Pneumonia, Tuberculosis, Pleural effusion, Pneumothorax
  • Gastroenterology: Hepatitis, Cirrhosis, Portal hypertension, Upper GI bleed, Pancreatitis
  • Nephrology: AKI, CKD, Nephrotic syndrome, Electrolyte disorders
  • Endocrinology: Diabetes mellitus, DKA, HHS, Thyroid disorders
  • Neurology: Stroke, Epilepsy, Meningitis, Parkinsonism
  • Hematology: Anemias, Leukemias, Coagulation disorders
  • Rheumatology: RA, SLE, Vasculitis

2. General Surgery

  • General Surgery: Shock, Burns, Fluid therapy, Wound healing, Hernia
  • GI Surgery: Acute abdomen, Intestinal obstruction, Appendicitis, Peritonitis
  • Hepatobiliary: Cholelithiasis, Obstructive jaundice
  • Urology: BPH, Renal stones
  • Thyroid & Breast: Hyperthyroidism, Thyroid cancers, Breast carcinoma
  • Trauma: Head injury, Polytrauma, Fractures

3. Obstetrics & Gynaecology

  • Antenatal Care: Physiological changes in pregnancy, ANC visits, Partograph
  • High-Risk Pregnancy: PIH, Preeclampsia, Eclampsia, GDM
  • Obstetric Emergencies: PPH, Shoulder dystocia, Abruptio placentae, Placenta previa
  • Gynaecology: Fibroid, AUB, Endometriosis, Infertility, Contraception, CIN, Carcinoma cervix

4. Pediatrics

  • Neonatology: Neonatal jaundice, HIE, Prematurity, NRP resuscitation
  • Nutrition & Infection: PEM, Diarrhea, Pneumonia, Measles, UIP Immunization schedule
  • Common Disorders: Congenital heart diseases, Nephrotic syndrome, Seizures

5. Preventive & Social Medicine (PSM - High Yield)

  • Epidemiology & Biostats: Study designs, Screening tests, Sensitivity/Specificity
  • National Health Programs: NTEP, HIV, NPCDCS, RBSK, HBNC, RMNCH+A, Immunization
  • Environment & Waste: Water purification, Biomedical waste management rules

6. Basic & Allied Medical Sciences

  • Pharmacology: ANS, CVS drugs, Diabetes drugs, ATT, Antibiotics, Antimalarials
  • Pathology & Micro: Cell injury, Neoplasia, Hematology, Sterilization, Virology, HIV
  • Anatomy & Physio: Nerve lesions, Cranial nerves, Histology, CVS/Renal physiology
  • Biochemistry & FMT: Metabolic cycles, Acid-base, IPC sections, Medical negligence, Toxicology
  • Short Specialties: ENT (CSOM, Epistaxis), Eye (Cataract, Glaucoma), Ortho (Fractures, Osteomyelitis), Skin, Psychiatry

💡 High-Yield Study Strategy for RUHS MO

  • Priority 1 (40–45 marks): Community Medicine (PSM) + General Medicine — master these two first.
  • Priority 2 (25–30 marks): Surgery + OBG — high yield, especially obstetric emergencies and surgical acute abdomen.
  • Priority 3 (15–20 marks): Pediatrics + Pharmacology — focus on immunization schedules, neonatology, and drug classes.
  • Priority 4 (10–15 marks): Short specialties (ENT/Ophthalmology/Ortho/Dermatology) — solve PYQs for pattern recognition.
  • PYQ Practice: Solve at least 3 previous year papers completely under timed conditions. Many questions repeat year-to-year.
  • Revision: Dedicate the last 2–3 weeks exclusively to short notes and rapid PYQ revision; avoid starting new topics.
